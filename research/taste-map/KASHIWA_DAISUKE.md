## INFERENCE — why this matters to Performable Music

The useful research question is not “how to sound like KASHIWA.”

It is:

> How can a small amount of melodic material sustain a long form while electronics, low-frequency weight, editing and density continuously reshape its meaning?

Potential characteristics worth testing:
- piano as an identity anchor while surrounding electronic structure changes;
- long accumulation and release rather than short loop-based development;
- contrast between fragile melodic material and massive electronic/low-frequency fields;
- form created through density, register, timbral violence, silence and recurrence;
- narrative continuity without requiring lyrics.

## MECHANISMS TO EXTRACT

### K01 — Fixed Piano / Variable Electronics
Keep one short original piano phrase stable. Change only:
- density;
- register doubling;
- spectral width;
- low-frequency weight;
- interruption / silence;
- spatial field.

### K02 — Recurrence With Changed Meaning
Return the same motif three times:
1. exposed;
2. buried/transformed;
3. released/recontextualized.

### K03 — Long-form Tension Curve
Do not think in 8-bar “drops.” Define large states:

```text
orientation → accumulation → fracture → suspension → return → release
```

### K04 — Piano / Noise Mass Boundary
Treat electronics as a physical field around the piano rather than accompaniment.

## PRACTICE TASK

Create an original 60–90 second study:

- one 4–8 note piano motif;
- no additional melody;
- three electronic states around it;
- one low-frequency event with structural meaning;
- one intentional silence;
- one return of the original motif.

Then make the electronic state live-controllable while the piano remains fixed.

## DO NOT COPY

Do not copy:
- melodies;
- chord progressions that function as a recognizable signature;
- recordings/stems;
- exact arrangements;
- distinctive sound-design sequences as a substitute for learning the mechanism.

Use legal listening copies for private analysis; do not commit copyrighted audio to this repository.
