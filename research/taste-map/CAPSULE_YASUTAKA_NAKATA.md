# Taste Map — CAPSULE / Yasutaka Nakata

**Status:** public-safe working research card.

Useful interview starting point:
- https://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2011/06/03/music/decade-of-fine-tuning-yields-gold-for-capsule/

Topics worth verifying from primary/long-form sources before formal publication:
- computer-centered / virtual-studio production;
- changing relationship between technopop, electro, club-oriented production and pop legibility;
- production constraints used as creative rules rather than merely technical limits.

## INFERENCE — why this matters to Performable Music

The useful lesson is not glossy Japanese pop production.

It is:

> A highly controlled digital studio can still produce clear musical identity when the constraints are strong enough and the arrangement remains immediately legible.

For this repository, CAPSULE/Nakata is useful as a counterweight to purely experimental systems:
- strong hooks and readable form;
- clean synthetic timbres;
- disciplined repetition;
- bright extended harmony;
- arrangement decisions that make dense electronic production understandable.

## MECHANISMS TO EXTRACT

### C01 — Constraint Game
Choose one severe production rule and finish the study anyway.

Examples:
- exactly 5 sound sources;
- one synth only;
- one BPM for the entire study;
- only one chord progression;
- only one drum kit;
- no new sound after bar 8.

### C02 — Pop Anchor / Experimental Field
Keep one clearly memorable element stable while the surrounding production becomes more experimental.

### C03 — Clean Density
Increase layers while preserving clear hierarchy and transient space.

### C04 — Bright Harmonic Colour
Use original Maj7/9/suspended/added-tone harmony as a colour field without copying known songs.

## PRACTICE TASK

Create a 60-second original track using:
- 5 sound sources maximum;
- 1 drum kit;
- 1 synth instrument;
- 1 harmonic loop;
- 1 clearly memorable motif;
- 3 arrangement states.

Then expose only two live parameters:
- `density`;
- `brightness`.

The piece should remain identifiable even when those parameters change.

## DO NOT COPY

Do not copy:
- recognizable melodies;
- signature vocal processing chains as a substitute for composition;
- exact drum patterns from a reference song;
- copyrighted recordings.

Study constraint, clarity and production hierarchy.

## ACKNOWLEDGEMENT MODEL

> Research influence: CAPSULE / Yasutaka Nakata's computer-centered electronic production and the use of strong constraints, synthetic clarity and pop legibility as compositional tools. Resulting studies use original material.

## OPEN QUESTIONS

- Which constraints create identity instead of merely making production harder?
- How much experimental variation can occur before the memorable anchor disappears?
- Can a clean pop-like structure remain genuinely performable rather than turning into backing-track playback?
