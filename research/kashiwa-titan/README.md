# KASHIWA Daisuke — TITAN Research

## Why this album

`TITAN` is a strong case study for **piano × electronics × bass × emergent musical form**.

KASHIWA Daisuke describes the album as feeling more “born” than “made”: rather than assembling instruments and sounds as separate materials, the sounds seemed to shape the music by themselves. He also emphasizes simple tonal elements, powerful electronic soundscapes, beautiful piano melodies, and the importance of low frequency reproduction.

This makes the album useful for studying a question central to this repository:

> How can a small set of sonic rules generate a large-scale form that remains emotionally legible and performable?

## Research axes

For each track, analyze:

1. **Form** — sections, recurrence, transition type, climax/release.
2. **Piano function** — foreground melody, ostinato, harmonic anchor, texture, silence.
3. **Electronic function** — pulse, noise, bass, distortion, spatial field, edit/cut.
4. **Low-frequency architecture** — when bass enters, how it changes perceived scale and form.
5. **Time** — BPM hypothesis, pulse stability, polymeter/irregularity, rubato.
6. **Density** — number of active layers and rate of events.
7. **Spectral movement** — brightness, noise/harmonic balance, register expansion.
8. **Emergence hypothesis** — what small rule or interaction might be generating the larger shape?
9. **Performability** — which 2–4 parameters could a performer control live without destroying the composition?

## Method

Use both listening and computation:

```text
legally obtained local audio
        ↓
Sonic Visualiser + manual annotations
        ↓
librosa / Essentia descriptors
        ↓
track timeline CSV/JSON
        ↓
mechanism hypothesis
        ↓
original Ableton / Max reconstruction
        ↓
practice reflection
```

Do not publish the source audio. Commit only annotations, derived features, diagrams and original reconstructions.
