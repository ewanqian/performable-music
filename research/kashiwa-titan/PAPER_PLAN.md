# Paper Plan — KASHIWA Daisuke, TITAN, and Performable Form

## Working title

**From Programmatic Form to Emergent Sound: Piano, Electronics, and Performable Structure in KASHIWA Daisuke’s _TITAN_**

## Core question

How does _TITAN_ create large-scale form from a small set of tonal, pianistic, electronic, and low-frequency processes, and which of those processes can be reconstructed as live-performable systems?

## Contribution

This should not be a fan essay and should not stop at genre labels. The contribution is a three-part method:

1. **close listening / formal analysis** of all eight tracks;
2. **computational audio analysis** of dynamics, spectral change, pulse/onsets, tonal movement and density;
3. **practice-based reconstruction** in Ableton / Max, testing whether hypothesized mechanisms remain musically meaningful when made performable.

## Proposed structure

### 1. Introduction
- Why _TITAN_ matters to performable electronic composition.
- The artist’s own “born rather than made” statement.
- Research questions and scope.

### 2. Lineage and context
Separate documented influence from analytical genealogy.

Documented KASHIWA context includes:
- Yodaka / post-rock origins;
- early solo combination of classical, electronica, field recording and glitch-like editing;
- jazz listening influencing instrumentation in _program music II_;
- Queen and Maurizio Bianchi named as major influences in an early interview;
- literary/programmatic form in _program music I_.

Analytical genealogy to test rather than assume:
- nineteenth-century program music / narrative form;
- post-rock long-form dynamics and accumulative form;
- 2000s Japanese electronica / post-classical editing culture;
- ambient / musique-concrète-like environmental layering;
- jazz instrumentation and phrasing;
- noise as structural material rather than decoration.

### 3. Method
- timestamped manual segmentation;
- piano/electronic/bass role coding;
- onset and tempo analysis;
- RMS / dynamic envelope;
- spectral centroid / bandwidth / flatness;
- chroma / tonal-centroid exploration where meaningful;
- density and register curves;
- section similarity / recurrence;
- reconstruction diary.

### 4. Track-by-track analysis
One section per track using `TRACK_ANALYSIS_TEMPLATE.md`.

### 5. Cross-album findings
Compare _TITAN_ with selected moments from:
- _april.#02_;
- _program music I_;
- _program music II_;
- _Ice_ where useful.

The comparison should focus on process, not on ranking albums.

### 6. Practice reconstruction
Build 3–4 original studies rather than covers:

- Study A — **Piano / Bass Emergence**
- Study B — **Cut / Accumulate / Release**
- Study C — **Low-frequency form**
- Study D — **Piano / electronic mutual shaping**

Each study should have only a few live controls and a saved deterministic state.

### 7. Discussion
- What “sounds shaping the music” can mean operationally.
- Difference between fixed composition and performable system.
- What transfers to Stage Casting / Performable Music without copying KASHIWA’s style.

### 8. Conclusion
- Findings about form, piano/electronic coupling and low-frequency architecture.
- Limits of computational descriptors.
- Future album / live-set implications.

## Evidence standard

For every claim, label internally as FACT / INFERENCE / EXPERIMENT.

Do not claim direct influence from a historical school unless the artist or a reliable source documents it. Genealogical comparisons can still be useful if explicitly framed as analytical parallels.

## Publishable research artifacts

- section/timestamp tables;
- feature CSV/JSON;
- original plots;
- arrangement graphs;
- original MIDI / Max patches / Ableton devices;
- brief lawful quotations and citations;
- reconstruction renders made from original material.

Do not publish full reference audio or a substitute transcription that reproduces the work.
