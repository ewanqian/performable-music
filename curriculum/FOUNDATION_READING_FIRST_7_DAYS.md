# Foundation Reading / Listening — First 7 Days

This is intentionally short. Finish these before collecting another hundred links.

## A. How to learn electronic music as a practice

### MIT — Music Technology / Electronic Music Composition
Read the course descriptions for:
- Introduction to Music Technology;
- Electronic Music Composition I / II;
- Interactive Music Systems;
- Algorithms and Interactions for Human-AI Music Making.

Start: https://music.mit.edu/focus-areas/music-technology/

**Extract:** weekly-study logic, synthesis/DSP/generative practice, critique, interactive systems, AI-human music questions.

### Princeton — Computer and Electronic Music + Live Electronic Music
Start: https://music.princeton.edu/academics/undergraduate-studies/courses

**Extract:** build instruments/interfaces, networking/HCI, perform regularly, critique works artistically and culturally.

### Berklee — Electronic Music Performance / Live Playback Engineering / Sound Design for Electronic Performance
Start:
- https://college.berklee.edu/courses/mti-682
- https://college.berklee.edu/courses/mtec-390
- https://college.berklee.edu/courses/mtec-312

**Extract:** gesture and control, personalized performance rig, rehearsal/troubleshooting, unique sonic voice.

### NYU — Multichannel Media Installation & Performance
https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/courses/multichannel-media-installation-performance

**Extract:** fixed / generative / reactive / performance-based / interactive systems belong to one continuum, with sensors, cameras and critique.

## B. KASHIWA Daisuke — form before style

### TITAN artist statement
https://kashiwadaisuke.com/2024/10/19/titan-kashiwa-daisuke-11th-new-album/

Question:
> What might “sounds themselves shaping the music” mean as an explicit system?

### program music I
https://www.noble-label.net/catalog/?code=CXCA-1212&en=

Question:
> How can a musical form tell a story without merely accompanying one?

### Listening task
Listen to one TITAN track and one `program music I` movement with a timeline sheet. Mark only:
- new state;
- recurrence;
- piano role;
- electronics role;
- bass/scale change;
- release.

Do not describe genre yet.

## D. Electronic-performance lineage

### YMO — pre-MIDI live systems
Read:
https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2014/11/yellow-magic-orchestra-gear/

Focus on the MC-8, click track, synchronization, overheating/failure and human musicians continuing through machine problems.

Question:
> What did the machine make possible, and what still had to remain human?

### CAPSULE
Official biography:
https://www.capsule-official.com/biography

Question:
> What changes when a computer becomes the whole virtual studio rather than one instrument inside the studio?

Optional interview:
https://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2011/06/03/music/decade-of-fine-tuning-yields-gold-for-capsule/

Pay attention to constraint as a game: a strict production limitation can produce identity.

## E. Embodied musical interfaces

### NIME 2025 — EMMA
https://nime.org/proc/nime2025_35/index.html

EMG + motion sensors treat fingers/hand/arm as expressive musical control.

### NIME 2026 — KinoGroove
https://nime.org/proc/nime2026_20/index.html

EMG complements visible motion by capturing muscular intensity that ordinary motion tracking may miss.

Question:
> What musical variable represents visible movement, and what variable represents internal effort?

This is a useful research branch for future work, but not required for Phase 01 unless hardware is already available.

# Seven-day task

Each day produce one page or artifact:

1. **Day 1** — Define Performability in your own words + one 60s one-button study.
2. **Day 2** — TITAN track timeline + original piano/electronic 60s reconstruction.
3. **Day 3** — Daito process map + one mathematical rhythm study.
4. **Day 4** — YMO/CAPSULE technology timeline + one constraint-based production study.
5. **Day 5** — Agent Patch diagram + one Agent-generated A/B/C arrangement test.
6. **Day 6** — NIME embodied interface notes + gesture/state mapping test.
7. **Day 7** — Listen to all six artifacts; keep two, reject four, write why.

The rejection step is part of the curriculum.
